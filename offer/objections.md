# Objections and the honest answer to each

**"We already have a billing company."**
Then ask them for your denial rate and your still-appealable dollar total by payer. If they can
produce both in a day, you probably don't need us. Most can't, because working denials is the least
profitable part of their contract. The audit is free and tells you which it is.

**"How much will we actually recover?"**
I don't know, and anyone who gives you a percentage before seeing your data is guessing at you.
The audit tells you what is *still appealable* — that number is computable from your file. What
each payer then does is their decision, not mine, and I won't price a promise on it.

**"Why is it free? What's the catch?"**
It costs me compute and no staff time, because it's automated. If it finds a big recoverable total
you'll probably want the Sprint. If it doesn't, I've told you your denials are under control, which
is worth knowing and costs you nothing. I won't sell you the Sprint if the audit finds under $7,500
appealable — that's a written rule, not a courtesy.

**"Can you just do it on contingency?"**
No. Two reasons and the first is mine: I don't take a percentage of a settlement, on principle.
The second is yours: contingency pricing means my incentive is volume of appeals filed, not quality,
and you'd be paying most for the claims that were easiest anyway.

**"$1,500 is a lot."**
Compare it to your own cost, not to zero. At 40 claims and the industry $25–$181 per-claim rework
cost, doing this in-house is $1,000–$7,240 of staff time — and the reason it hasn't happened is
that your staff don't have the hours. If the audit shows less than $7,500 recoverable, I'll tell
you not to buy it.

**"Send me your case studies."**
I don't have any yet — you'd be early. I won't show you someone else's results with my name on
them. What I can do is run the audit on your data for free, and you can judge the analysis itself.

**"Is our patient data safe?"**
You never send it. The export is de-identified — no names, no DOB, no member IDs — and the system
**refuses the file** if identifier columns are present. Claim-level data only.

**"Who submits the appeals?"**
You do. I prepare the packets — letter, payer route, deadline, documentation checklist. Submitting
on your behalf would need access to your systems and identified patient data; this offer is built
to avoid both.

**"We're too busy right now."**
The export takes about ten minutes to run. Understood if the answer's no — but some of these claims
have a timely-filing deadline, and after it passes the money is gone permanently rather than
delayed. That's the only urgency here and it's real.
