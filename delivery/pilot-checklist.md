# Pilot delivery checklist

The $500 pilot: work a contractor's last 90 days of unsold estimates, by hand, over 3 weeks.
No software required. The point is to learn what delivery actually costs you before automating it.

---

## Before taking payment — agree in writing

Ambiguity about what was sold is *gharar*, and it is also how engagements end badly.
Put these in a one-page agreement and have them confirm by email.

- [ ] **Scope**: unsold estimates from the last 90 days, one trade, one location.
- [ ] **Duration**: 3 weeks of follow-up from the day the list is received.
- [ ] **Fee**: $500, flat, paid up front. No percentage, no fee tied to their financing products.
- [ ] **Definition of a recovered job**: *a job the client confirms closed, from a contact in the
      supplied list, that had gone quiet before follow-up began.* Agree this wording explicitly —
      it is the number the renewal will be argued over.
- [ ] **Approval**: client approves message copy before anything sends.
- [ ] **Sending identity**: messages go out under the client's brand, from the client's number/domain.
- [ ] **What you are not doing**: not cold-contacting anyone new, not touching their ad accounts,
      not promoting financing offers.
- [ ] **Refund position**: state plainly what happens if nothing closes. Recommended — offer the
      pilot fee back if zero jobs are recovered. It costs little, it makes the yes far easier,
      and it keeps you honest about who you sell to.

## Intake

- [ ] Export unsold estimates. Where to find them:
  - **ServiceTitan** — Estimates report, filter status `Open`/`Dismissed`
  - **Jobber** — Quotes, filter `Awaiting Response`
  - **Housecall Pro** — Estimates, status `Pending`
  - **No CRM** — ask for the quote folder or spreadsheet; if it's verbal only, refuse the pilot
- [ ] Required columns: customer name, phone, email, estimate date, amount, job type, status.
- [ ] Strip anything already won, lost-for-cause, or duplicated.
- [ ] Note age of each estimate — under 60 days converts far better; sort accordingly.
- [ ] **Confirm consent basis**: these are the client's own prior customers. Confirm the client has
      an existing business relationship with each. Anyone who previously opted out stays out.
- [ ] Agree the calendar: where do booked appointments land, and who confirms them?

## Weekly rhythm

| Week | You do | They do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Touches 1–2, log every reply | Approve copy, take booked calls |
| 2 | Touches 3–4, hand off warm replies same day | Quote the revived jobs |
| 3 | Touch 5, compile attribution log | Confirm what actually closed |

- [ ] Hand every warm reply to the client **the same day**. A revived lead going cold on your desk
      is worse than never having contacted them.
- [ ] Log every outcome in `attribution-log.csv` as it happens, not at the end.

## Close-out

- [ ] Sit down with the client and go through `attribution-log.csv` line by line.
- [ ] Report the real number, including zero. If the pilot failed, say it failed and say why —
      wrong list, estimates too old, pricing problem, wrong trade.
- [ ] Only then make the retainer offer: $1,000/mo, ongoing follow-up on new estimates as they go out.
- [ ] Ask for the referral regardless of outcome: *"who else do you know who quotes a lot and chases little?"*
- [ ] Record your own hours. If the pilot took 25 hours, the retainer needs automation before client #4.

## What to automate first (decide from real data, not now)

Track these during the pilot and let them choose the roadmap:

- Which step ate the most hours?
- Which touch produced the most replies?
- What did you retype more than three times?
