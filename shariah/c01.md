# Shariah analysis — c01 Denial recovery / A-R sprint for US independent practices
**Verdict: PASSES. Certainty 8/10.** Conditions attached — see §9.

## 1. What exactly is sold, and what exactly is the consideration?
Sold: a defined administrative work product. Specifically (a) a free **Denial Recovery Audit** —
an analysis of the practice's own denial data identifying which denied and underpaid claims are
still appealable, why they were denied, and what each is worth; and (b) a paid **Denial Recovery
Sprint** — a fixed-scope, fixed-duration engagement producing prepared appeal packets and a
corrected-resubmission worklist for a stated set of claims.
Consideration: a **flat fee in money, fixed in advance** (£/$1,500 for the Sprint as the operator's
existing offer stands). This is *ijārah* / a contract for defined services, not a partnership in
proceeds.

## 2. Is the object of sale sufficiently specified (gharar)?
It must be, and the offer is written so that it is: the deliverable is enumerated by artefact and
by count, the claim set is bounded (denials within a stated date range, above a stated dollar
floor), the duration is fixed, and the price does not vary with outcome. What is deliberately
**not** promised is a recovery amount — the payer's decision is outside our control, and promising
it would be precisely the *gharar* to avoid. The guarantee is therefore attached to the work
product and the analysis, not to the payer's behaviour. See `offer/offer.md` §Guarantee.

## 3. Riba
- **Fee structure:** flat, pre-agreed. This is the decisive point. A **contingency percentage of
  recovered funds is rejected**, both because the fee would be unknown at contract (gharar) and
  because it makes our earnings a share in the proceeds of an insurance settlement rather than a
  price for defined labour. The industry norm is contingency; we take the conservative position
  and forgo it.
- **Payment rails:** the customer pays us by card or bank transfer. We neither offer nor arrange
  instalment credit, financing, factoring, or "pay from recoveries" terms — all of which would
  either be interest-bearing or would convert the fee into a debt trade.
- **Do we push the customer into interest-bearing credit?** No. No financing partner, no referral
  fee from any lender, no "finance this engagement" option. If the practice cannot pay the fee, the
  answer is a smaller scope, not credit.
- **Conventional health insurance in the chain.** This is the genuine point of scholarly
  difference and the reason certainty is 8 and not 10. Many scholars hold conventional insurance
  impermissible (*gharar* and *riba* in the insurer's own contracts and reserves). Our analysis:
  we are not a party to the insurance contract, we do not sell insurance, we do not earn from the
  insurer, and we take no share of the settlement. We are paid a fixed price by a physician for
  administrative work that recovers money **already earned by treating patients** and wrongly
  withheld. The money is the physician's *ujra* for lawful medical work. Recovering a debt owed
  for lawful services is permissible even where the debtor's own business model is questionable.
  Conservative note recorded: a scholar taking the strictest view of any involvement with
  conventional insurers would object. We proceed, disclose the reasoning here, and would drop the
  candidate if the operator's own scholar advises otherwise.

## 4. Maysir
No. Revenue does not depend on chance. The fee is fixed and earned on delivery of the work
product regardless of whether any given appeal succeeds. Note that a contingency model **would**
introduce a chance element — a second reason to reject it.

## 5. Rights to every asset, dataset, API and piece of content
- Claims data: supplied by the client, under a written processing instruction, used only to
  perform the engagement. We do not retain, resell, aggregate or train on it.
- NPPES provider data (for prospecting): a US public-record dataset published by CMS for public
  use. **Not accessible in this session** — see the constraint file. Nothing is used that we have
  not lawfully obtained.
- CARC/RARC denial code definitions: published code lists maintained for industry use. We
  reproduce the meaning of a code in an audit; we do not republish the code set as a product.
- Software: written in this repository, no dependencies. Model use is our own licensed access.

## 6. Are all claims literally true?
Enforced, not intended. The audit reports only what is computed from the client's own file. No
borrowed case studies, no invented recovery percentages, no "clients typically recover X" unless
X is our own logged, real result — and at present we have none, so the offer says so. The outreach
artefact cites a specific verifiable observation about that practice or states a general statistic
**with its source named in the message**. `system/` contains no template that can emit an
unsourced performance claim.

## 7. Harm surface
- **To the customer:** low. Worst case they pay a fixed fee and recover less than it. Mitigated by
  the free audit running *first* — the audit tells them the size of the opportunity before they
  spend anything, and if the recoverable total does not clearly exceed the fee, we say so and do
  not sell. That refusal is implemented, not promised: see `system/score/`.
- **To patients (third parties):** this is the real surface. Two hard rules follow. (i) The audit
  runs on **de-identified claim-level data only** — no patient name, DOB, address, MRN or member
  ID. The ingest strips and refuses identifier columns rather than trusting the sender. (ii) We
  never advise or perform balance-billing a patient for an amount the payer denied. The service
  recovers money from the payer, never from the patient.
- **To payers:** none beyond being asked to honour claims they are contractually obliged to pay
  through the appeal channel they themselves publish. We do not fabricate documentation, alter
  dates of service, upcode, or appeal claims that were correctly denied. A claim the audit finds
  legitimately denied is reported as legitimately denied.

## 8. Is the customer's own core revenue impermissible?
No. The customer is a medical practice; its core revenue is treating patients. **Exclusion list
applied at prospecting time** and enforced in code: no practice whose primary revenue is
cosmetic-only elective work marketed deceptively, fertility services involving third-party gametes,
gender-clinic-only practices, abortion-focused clinics, or any practice under active fraud
investigation. Behavioural health, primary care, and standard specialty practices are in scope.

## 9. Conditions on the pass
1. Flat fee only. If the operator ever switches to contingency, this analysis no longer holds and
   the candidate must be re-screened.
2. De-identified data only for the audit tier.
3. No financing, no lender referral, no interest on late payment of our own invoice.
4. No claim of results we have not produced and logged.
