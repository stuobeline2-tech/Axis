# Shariah analysis — c13 UK SME invoice chasing / A-R recovery
**Verdict: KILLED. Certainty 5/10.** Excluded despite a weighted score of 6.20, which was third
highest overall and above three candidates that remain eligible. This is the gate doing its job.

## 1. What is sold / consideration
Sold: chasing a client's overdue sales ledger. Consideration: either a flat fee (clean) or —
overwhelmingly the market norm — a percentage of sums recovered.

## 2. Gharar
A percentage-of-recovery fee is unknown at contract. Under a flat fee this is curable.

## 3. Riba — the killing objection
The standard, and in the UK the statutorily encouraged, mechanic of commercial debt chasing is the
**Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998**: the creditor's principal lever is a claim
to statutory interest (Bank of England base rate + 8%) plus fixed compensation. An effective
invoice-chasing service *uses that lever* — the demand letters cite the accruing interest, and the
service's value is substantially the credibility of that threat. Building a business whose core
work product is a demand for interest is direct facilitation of *riba*, regardless of who receives
it. A "principal only, never mention interest" variant is possible but it discards the main
commercial lever, which means we would be selling a materially weaker service while competitors
sell the strong one — and the evidence in `research/evidence/c13.md` gives no reason to think
buyers would pay comparable money for it.

Second riba leg: the adjacent and heavily marketed product in this space is **invoice finance**
(one retrieved source is literally about UK SMEs using invoice finance to beat late payment).
The commercial gravity of this niche pulls towards referring clients to interest-bearing
receivables financing. §2.2 says: where a serious concern survives analysis, kill the candidate —
do not mitigate it.

## 4. Maysir
Percentage-of-recovery introduces a chance element. Under flat fee, no.

## 5. Rights to assets
The client's own ledger, supplied under instruction. No issue.

## 6. Claims true
Achievable.

## 7. Harm surface
Moderate and real: debt chasing directed at other small businesses, with escalating pressure and
the interest threat as its instrument. Third-party harm is the debtor SME.

## 8. Customer's core revenue
Ordinary UK SMEs — generally permissible.

## Decision
Killed on question 3. Not mitigated, not restructured, not deferred. Recorded here so that a
future run does not rediscover it and reach a different answer by ignoring the interest mechanic.
